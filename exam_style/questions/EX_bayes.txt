A factory has three machines. Machine A makes 40% of the items, machine B makes 35% and machine C makes 25%. The fractions of defective items are 1% for A, 3% for B and 4% for C. --- A*
0.4082
>> Right. P(defective) = 0.4(0.01) + 0.35(0.03) + 0.25(0.04) = 0.0245, and P(C | defective) = 0.25(0.04)/0.0245 = 0.01/0.0245 = 0.4082.
--- B
0.4286
>> This is P(B | defective) = 0.0105/0.0245, the posterior of the wrong machine. Machine B contributes 0.0105 and machine C contributes 0.0100, which is why the two are close.
--- C

>> This is what you get by ignoring the production shares: 0.04/(0.01 + 0.03 + 0.04) = 0.5. The prior (how much each machine makes) must multiply the defect rate.
--- D
0.1633
>> This is P(A | defective) = 0.004/0.0245. Machine A makes many items but has a low defect rate, so it is the smallest share of the defective items, not machine C's.
--- explain
Total probability gives the denominator: P(def) = 0.4(0.01) + 0.35(0.03) + 0.25(0.04) = 0.004 + 0.0105 + 0.010 = 0.0245. Bayes: P(C | def) = 0.010/0.0245 = 0.4082. (The three posteriors are 0.1633, 0.4286, 0.4082 and add to 1.)
