--- stem
A telecom analyst wants to predict a customer's monthly bill. From her 2000 training customers she builds 50 new training sets of 2000 customers each, always drawing customers at random **with replacement**. She fits one regression tree to each set and predicts a new customer's bill as the **average** of the 50 trees' predictions. This ensemble method is known as:
--- A*
Bagging
>> Bootstrap samples of the rows, one model per sample, combined by averaging (a vote for classification): that is bootstrap aggregation.
--- B
Random forest
>> A random forest would also give each tree a random subset of the features. Here every tree sees all the features, so it is plain bagging.
--- C
Boosting
>> Boosting trains the models one after another, each focusing on the earlier models' errors. Here the 50 trees are built independently.
--- D
Bootstrapping
>> Bootstrapping is just resampling, usually to measure the spread of a statistic. Here models are trained on the resamples and combined, which makes it bagging.
--- explain
The key details: resampling rows with replacement (bootstrap), one model per resample (trained independently, so in parallel), and the models combined by average or vote. No feature sampling, so not a random forest. No weights or sequence, so not boosting. Answer: bagging.

--- stem
Sam trains small decision trees with a single split, one after another, on the same 500 customers. After each tree, the customers it got wrong are given larger weights and the others smaller weights, and the next tree is trained on the re-weighted data. At the end, the trees vote, and a tree with a lower weighted error gets a bigger say. This ensemble method is known as:
--- A
Bagging
>> Bagging resamples the rows and trains the models independently, with equal votes. It does not re-weight examples or train in sequence.

AdaBoost
>> Sequential weak learners, weights that favour previous mistakes, and a weighted vote: this is adaptive boosting.
